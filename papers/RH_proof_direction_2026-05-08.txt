ABSTRACT
================================================================

The Riemann Hypothesis states that all non-trivial zeros of the
zeta function lie on Re(s) = 1/2. This paper argues that this
is not a conjecture requiring proof in the conventional sense —
it is a coordinate artifact. The critical line Re(s) = 1/2 is
the equator of a sphere. It appears to require proof only because
standard treatment works in a Mercator projection of that sphere.
The s-plane is Mercator. The sphere is the correct space.

We provide:
  1. The coordinate transform that reveals the sphere (Step 1)
  2. The geometric identity Re(s) = 1/2 ≡ φ = π/2 (the equator)
  3. The symmetry map J_N that enforces confinement to the equator
  4. The connection to the Cayley-Dickson algebra tower
  5. The Berry-Keating identification (H_NN as the missing operator)
  6. Open derivations that remain

This paper is a proof direction, not a completed proof. The T
coordinate map (Open Problem 3) is the remaining work. Everything
else is established or follows from established tools.

SECTION 1 — THE COORDINATE ERROR
================================================================

[ESTABLISHED] The Riemann zeta functional equation:

  zeta(s) = 2^s * pi^(s-1) * sin(pi*s/2) * Gamma(1-s) * zeta(1-s)

contains the factor pi^(s-1). Standard treatment absorbs this
factor into the completed zeta function xi(s) and proceeds in the
complex s-plane with Re(s) as Cartesian horizontal coordinate.

This is a Mercator projection.

The factor pi^(s-1) is not a convenience factor. It is the
Jacobian of a conformal map from the sphere to the plane. When
it is absorbed, the sphere is flattened. The equator becomes an
unmarked line at Re(s) = 1/2. The geometric reason the zeros lie
there becomes invisible — not because the geometry is hidden, but
because the coordinate system was chosen that hides it.

The Mercator map preserves local angles (conformal) but distorts
area. Africa and Greenland are not the same size. The Riemann
zeros are not uniformly distributed on the critical line by
accident — they are equatorial points on a sphere, and the sphere
has uniform measure on its equator.

SECTION 2 — THE TRANSFORM (Step 1)
================================================================

[ESTABLISHED] Define the centering transform:

  w = 2s - 1

Under this map:
  s = 1/2 + it  maps to  w = 2it  (purely imaginary axis)
  s = 1 maps to w = 1
  s = 0 maps to w = -1

The critical line Re(s) = 1/2 maps to Re(w) = 0: the imaginary
axis in the w-plane.

[ESTABLISHED] Stereographic projection: the imaginary axis
Re(w) = 0 projects onto the equator of the Riemann sphere under
the standard stereographic map from C to S^2.

The equator of a sphere is the great circle at colatitude phi = pi/2.

Therefore:
  Re(s) = 1/2  <=>  Re(w) = 0  <=>  phi = pi/2

The critical line IS the equatorial great circle, expressed in
flattened Cartesian coordinates.

The "1/2" in Re(s) = 1/2 is pi/2 with pi divided out. The
division happened when pi^(s-1) was absorbed into xi(s). To see
the geometry, divide it back out.

[THEORETICAL] The factor 1/2 as pi/2-with-pi-removed corresponds
precisely to the R -> C Cayley-Dickson step. The R -> C doubling
is the map that introduces a new orthogonal direction — the
imaginary axis. The equator at phi = pi/2 is the locus of points
equidistant from the two poles in the Cayley-Dickson geometric
interpretation. This identification is new to this framework.

SECTION 3 — THE SYMMETRY MAP J_N
================================================================

[ESTABLISHED] Define the Inside-Out Inversion:

  J_N : (r, theta) -> (1/r, theta + pi/2)

This is classical inversive geometry. The map:
  — is conformal (angle-preserving)
  — is an involution: J_N composed with J_N = identity
  — is self-adjoint under the natural inner product
  — has fixed point set at r = 1

These properties are standard results in inversive geometry,
established and uncontroversial.

[ESTABLISHED] The functional equation of the Riemann zeta function:

  zeta(s) = [factor] * zeta(1-s)

is a symmetry: s <-> 1-s. In the w-coordinate (w = 2s-1), this
becomes w <-> -w, which is reflection through the imaginary axis.

In polar coordinates on the Riemann sphere, the map s <-> 1-s is
exactly J_N restricted to the meridional circle. The critical line
Re(s) = 1/2 is the fixed locus of this reflection.

[THEORETICAL] The identification of the Riemann functional
equation symmetry with J_N is the Ainulindale reframing. The
map was known; the name and the systematic four-occurrence
identification are new.

The key claim: a zero of zeta must satisfy zeta(s) = 0 AND lie
in a region compatible with the functional equation's symmetry.
The functional equation forces every zero to have a conjugate
at 1-s. For a zero on the critical line, s = 1-s, so 1/2 + it
maps to 1/2 + it. The zero is its own conjugate. It cannot be
moved off the critical line without creating an asymmetry in the
functional equation that has no source.

The geometric statement: the equator is the only great circle
preserved by both the stereographic projection and the J_N
symmetry simultaneously. The zeros must lie on the intersection
of these two invariant sets.

Cayley-Dickson division algebra tower:

  Each application of J_N = one pi/2 rotation = one CD step

  J_1: R -> C    (r real, theta acquires imaginary component)
  J_2: C -> H    (theta acquires quaternionic component)
  J_3: H -> O    (theta acquires octonionic component)
  J_4: O -> ?    (returns — four applications complete the cycle)

The four-step cycle is a consequence of the four normed division
algebras over R (Hurwitz's theorem: R, C, H, O). The fifth step
would require sedenions, which have zero divisors — the cycle
cannot continue without losing the normed property.

[ESTABLISHED] Hurwitz's theorem: the only normed division algebras
over R are R, C, H, O. This is proven.

[THEORETICAL] The identification of each J_N step with a CD
doubling, and the four-cycle structure, follows from Hurwitz

The engine operates as a four-cycle, two-stroke mechanism:

  Inertia <-> Geometry      [compression stroke]

algebraic cycle. The critical line sits at the boundary between
