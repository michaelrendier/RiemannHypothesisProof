Background 5.1 — Chladni's Node Line Theorem   [ESTABLISHED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ernst Chladni demonstrated in 1787 that a vibrating plate bowed at a specific
frequency produces standing waves whose node lines — loci of zero amplitude —
form characteristic geometric patterns (Chladni figures). Sand placed on the
plate migrates to the node lines, where the plate is stationary.

  Chladni, E.F.F. (1787). Entdeckungen über die Theorie des Klanges.
  Leipzig: Weidmanns Erben und Reich.

The mathematical foundation: node lines are the zero sets of eigenfunctions
of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the resonating surface. Their geometry
is determined entirely by the symmetry of the surface and the angular momentum
quantum number of the excited mode.

Theorem 5.2 — Courant's Nodal Domain Theorem   [ESTABLISHED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The k-th eigenfunction of the Laplacian on a bounded domain has at most k
nodal domains. For the fundamental mode (k=1), there is exactly one node
— one locus dividing the surface into two nodal domains.

  Courant, R., and Hilbert, D. (1953). Methods of Mathematical Physics,
  Volume I. Interscience Publishers. §VI.6.

On the Riemann sphere S², the fundamental mode is the spherical harmonic:

    Y_1^0(θ, φ) = cos(θ)

Its unique node line is cos(θ) = 0, i.e., θ = π/2 — the equatorial great
circle.

    l=0, m=0:  no node line (constant function)

    l=2, m=0:  two node lines at θ = arccos(±1/√3)
