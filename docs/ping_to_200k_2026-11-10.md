## 1 Correction to log item 25
The fixed threshold ½ ln 2 overstated the loss at large n. When the zero sum stops resolving, every integer reads ≈ 1 (the smooth slope of ψ), so "false alarm 1.000" there only says the threshold is below the baseline. The right measure is how well the recovered jump ranks primes above non-prime-powers: balanced accuracy of the best single threshold, and AUC.

Perfect classification ends near n ≈ 1,000 at 3,066 zero-tones and n ≈ 10,000 at 25,000 (8.15 × the zero-tones, ≈ 10 × the n): the edge moves a little faster than linearly with K (edges read from blocks, ±30 %; not a fit). Beyond it the ping decays smoothly, it does not fall off a cliff. The loss is the truncation error of the explicit formula, which grows with x and shrinks with T (Gonek's uniform form: O(x ln(2xT) ln ln 3x / T)); that is the literature account, not something these numbers pin down.

## 3 Where the 65 wheels work (the sieve side: `rhp/wheel65.py` and the stragglers equation in LOG_2026-11-07)
Exact for every n < 100,489 = 317² (exhaustive): no wheel rings ⇔ prime. 313 is the 65th prime. Above 317² the stragglers (composites with no prime factor ≤ 313) appear and the 65 wheels read them as primes; their share of the survivors is 1 − 1/(Uω(U)) (Buchstab), measured 0.108 … 0.366 for X = 2·10⁵ … 10⁷.
The two limits are of different resources: the sieve side is limited by how many wheels (slots) are fired; the wave side by how many zero-tones (bandwidth). They are complementary: between 10⁴ and 10⁵ the 65 wheels are exact while the 25,000-zero-tone ping is at 0.70 – 0.99; below 10³ both are exact.

## 4 Are the stragglers the cause of the zero-side fuzziness? Measured: no
At n ≥ 100,489 the stragglers read slightly LOWER than other composites (mean recovered jump 0.79 against 0.96 at 25,000 zero-tones, Cohen d = −0.31 and −0.35; 0.98 against 1.00 at 3,066, d = −0.09 and −0.23); over all composites corr(recovered jump, ln smallest prime factor) = −0.12 (25,000) and −0.06 (3,066). The blur of the zero sum does not depend on factor structure to first order: stragglers do not look more prime-like to it.

## 5 Where 1 sits, and the tower
- The empty Euler product is 1: the path starts at log P₀ = 0 at x = 1, before prime 2 moves it; n = 1 is the constant 1 in ζ = 1 + T + L (neither tree); ψ(x) = 0 on [1, 2) so the prime wave is the plain ramp −√x there. In the algebra the identity is e₀, slot 0: the point all the circles share. The seq1 rule (k-th prime on slot k ≥ 1) leaves slot 0 for 1. The char rule maps 1 → 0 (trivial character) and also every prime in the trivial class (≈ 1/N of them), colliding with 1: it needs a modification (an extra bit) to keep slot 0 for 1 alone. The wiki-130 table puts prime 2 on slot 0.
- Any injective rule needs T_128 for the 65 wheels: 63 imaginary slots of T_64 hold the primes up to 307 (seq1); 313 is the 65th prime. A rule that is not injective (mod, char) needs no larger T_N.
- The tower as a ratchet in u = ln x: the first prime that needs the next level under seq1 is the N-th prime: 53, 131, 311, 719 for N = 16, 32, 64, 128 (u = 3.970, 4.875, 5.740, 6.578; steps 0.905, 0.865, 0.838 → ln 2 as the prime count doubles per level). Against the ζ loop (2π/t in u): per level step ≈ 2.0 / 1.9 loops at t = γ₁, ≈ 14 / 13 loops at t = 100. This is arithmetic of π(x); whether the tower levels themselves move is not something these tests can see (E1/E2 of the slot-rule test found no structure that selects a slot assignment).
