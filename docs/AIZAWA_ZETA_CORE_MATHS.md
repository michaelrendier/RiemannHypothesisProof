# The core maths of the Aizawa pathway, and what it says about the zeta pathway

Written 2026-11-24 for Cody. Every number below was measured in this repository (experiments or tests named in each row); nothing here is evidence about the Riemann Hypothesis.
Code: `ValaQuenta/modules/aizawa_attractor`, `ValaQuenta/modules/zeta_uns_path`, `experiments/diagonal_quadratics.py`, viewer `visualizations/javascript/webgl_aizawa_vs_zeta_uns.html`.

## 1. What the Aizawa path is

The system dx/dt = (z−b)x − dy, dy/dt = dx + (z−b)y, dz/dt = c + az − z³/3 − (x²+y²)(1+ez) + f z x³ looks like a tangle. In cylindrical coordinates (r, φ, z) the first two equations are exact:

    dr/dt = (z − b) r        dφ/dt = d

The orbit **turns about the z axis at a constant rate d**, z sets the **growth rate of the radius**, and r can never reach the axis. Only the last term (f z x³) breaks the rotation symmetry; at f = 0 the 3-D orbit is the 2-D (r, z) flow swept round the axis (checked to 1.7×10⁻⁸). The fixed points sit on the axis with eigenvalues (z*−b) ± id and a − z*². The largest Lyapunov exponent is +0.085. A consequence worth keeping: ln r stays bounded on the attractor, so the long-time average of d(ln r)/dt = z − b is exactly zero, i.e. **⟨z⟩ = b** (measured 0.701 against b = 0.7 over 60,000 points; the orbit has z > b, radius growing, 48% of the time and z < b, radius shrinking, 52%). r and z are uncorrelated (−0.003) and mean z is the same in the tube, the middle and the shell, so the tube (a third of the time at r < 0.35) is not picked out by a value of z. The zeta analogue is the radial law ⟨Z′/Z⟩ ≈ 0 in the same bounded-log sense.

## 2. The same skeleton in zeta, and where it differs

Hardy: ζ(½+it) = e^(−iθ(t)) Z(t), Z real. So the path **turns at −θ′(t) = −½ ln(t/2π)** with a **signed radius Z(t)**.

| | Aizawa | ζ on the critical line | measured here |
|---|---|---|---|
| rotation rate | d, constant | −θ′(t), slowly rising | identity, residual 10⁻¹⁰ |
| radial law | (dr/dt)/r = z − b | (d/dt) ln\|Z\| = Z′/Z | Z′/Z = Σ over zeros 2t/(t²−γ²) + smooth: spread 0.573 → 0.007 away from the zeros (25,000 zeros); the remainder is a near-constant 0.75 |
| the axis | never reached | passed through at every zero (Z changes sign) | sign change at each of the first 30 zeros |
| driver | z, a third variable | the zeros themselves (poles of Z′/Z) | as above |

So "the same animation from the pole" is true of the skeleton and not of the picture: down the axis Aizawa is concentric spirals around a hole; ζ in the value plane is lobes that go through the centre, because its radius is signed. The spring is the UNS cylinder (ρ = log₁₀|ζ| + 3, φ = arg ζ, height = t) with the zeros on its axis.

**A lesson from the first render.** Sampling by equal steps of θ puts every point on 32 fixed angles of the flat point, which draws 32 rigid spokes. That was a sampling artefact. A golden-ratio jitter on the phase grid removes it (tested).

## 3. Where ½ comes from as a phase

The functional equation ζ(s) = χ(s) ζ(1−s) has |χ(½+it)| = 1 and χ = e^(−2iθ(t)): on the line it is a **pure phase**, and Z is what remains when that phase is taken out. σ does not rotate anything: in each term p^(−s) = p^(−σ) e^(−it ln p) the **t part is the phase** and the σ part is the **amplitude**; ½ is where the amplitude p^(−σ) of the series and p^(σ−1) of its mirror agree. The axis of rotation is therefore the **t (imaginary) direction**, not the σ axis; σ moves the radius.

## 5. The diagonals, as phase offsets, and the orthogonal wave

A diagonal of the Ulam spiral is a quadratic f(n). Shifting along it, n → n+δ, is a **phase offset in n**; it changes b and c but **keeps the discriminant** Δ = b² − 4c. The prime density depends on Δ through the **character (Δ/p)**:

| claim | result |
|---|---|
| the number of roots of f mod p is 1 + (Δ/p) (p odd, p ∤ Δ) | 2,267 of 2,267 cases |
| shifts of Euler's n²+n+41 (all Δ = −163) give the same density | counts 2,261 … 2,234 over 12 shifts: spread 9, against 47 for random noise (the slow drift is 1/ln f(n) as the window moves out) |
| Bateman–Horn predicts the counts across 120 random quadratics | r = 0.9994; observed/predicted median 0.996, 5–95% range 0.94–1.05 over the 89 with a nonzero prediction; the 31 with a fixed prime divisor are predicted 0 and produced 2 primes; Euler's 41: observed 2,261, predicted 2,266.8 |

So the "other wave orthogonal to the numbers" is the **Dirichlet character χ_Δ(p) = (Δ/p)**. It is orthogonal to the trivial character that gives ζ, it has its own L-function L(s, χ_Δ) with its own critical line, and a diagonal's prime density is L(1, χ_Δ) read off through Bateman–Horn. Polynomials with a large L(1, χ_Δ) (Δ = −163 for Euler's) are the dense diagonals. Bateman–Horn is a conjecture (1962); 0.9994 is agreement with it, not a proof.

## 6. The torus, and the gradient mesh

The CSS "Gradient Mesh" (#1074 in the collection) is nine radial gradients on a 3 × 3 grid of control points, summed with soft edges: a smooth field interpolated from control values (a radial-basis mixture; the vector-graphics version is a Coons or bicubic patch). The torus that does appear in this work is the **prime-phase torus**: the phases (t ln p₁, t ln p₂, …) mod 2π. For two primes the path (t ln 2, t ln 3) covers 100% of a 16 × 16, 32 × 32 and 64 × 64 grid of that torus (Kronecker: ln 2/ln 3 is irrational), and the two-prime Euler factors are a function on it, which is exactly a mesh. The UNS cylinder is around the t axis; it becomes a torus only when t is wrapped for finitely many primes. The strip-to-sphere chart (θ = πσ) is a sphere, not a torus.

## 8. The sparse side, the pseudo-cardioid, and the Ulam diagonals (2026-11-24, second pass; `experiments/pole_density_and_ulam.py`)

**The sparse side seen from the pole.** The mean value of ζ(½+it) over t is 1.0000 at every zero count (the n = 1 term), so the path leans to Re ζ > 0. The share of the path with Re ζ < 0 is 18% (100 zeros), 21% (1,000), 23% (5,000), **24.0% (25,000)**: between a fifth and a quarter. The least-dense direction is the negative real axis (−177.5°) at every count. It is not a truncation: the n = 1 term alone is a circle through 0 that never enters Re < 0, the primes and prime powers push the path into Re < 0, and the width of log ζ grows only like √(½ ln ln t) (Selberg 1946), so the sparse side fills very slowly with height; more primes at the same heights would not remove it.

**The pseudo-cardioid.** ζ = S + e^(−2iθ) S̄ + R with S = Σ_{n≤N} n^(−½−it), N = ⌊√(t/2π)⌋ (exact algebra of the Riemann–Siegel sum; residual ≤ (t/2π)^(−1/4), measured ratio to the bound ≤ 0.92). So the path is the prime spring S plus its mirror turning at −2θ′. N = 1 gives a circle through 0; adding n ≥ 2 gives the pseudo-cardioid. A true cardioid has a cusp where the speed is 0; ζ crosses 0 with speed |Z′| ≥ 0.62 at each of the first 1,500 zeros (median 3.77), so there is no cusp.

**The Ulam diagonals.** In a 301 × 301 square spiral there are 2,239 straight diagonal pieces (length ≥ 8). Every one has second difference exactly 8, so every piece is f(k) = 4k² + bk + c, which is the perfect squares displaced by a constant: f = 4x² − D/16 with x = k + b/8 and D = b² − 16c (identity error 4×10⁻⁹).
- A **whole (central) diagonal**: small |D|; D = 0 is the squares themselves (b = 4, c = 1: (2k+1)², no primes).
- The **short diagonals where primes sit**: pieces of non-central diagonal lines, each with its own D. The richest in this spiral: D = −652 (86 primes in 131 terms, two pieces with the same D and different (b, c): phase offsets of each other), D = −12 (74 in 301), D = 272 (72 in 142). Prime richness follows the character (D/p), as in §5.
- **What they are a shadow of.** In the Sacks picture (radius √m, angle 2π√m) a diagonal is a ray at the fixed angle **πb/2 (mod 2π)**, because the angle step 2π√4 = 4π ≡ 0; it bends by **−πD/(32k)**. Measured: err × k = 1.5708 for D = −16 and 1.1778 for D = −12, against predicted 1.5708 and 1.1781; D = 0 is exact. So a diagonal is the shadow of the squares of the even numbers, displaced by −D/16, wound onto the plane at the resonance angle where √a is an integer. A phase offset in k keeps D; moving to a parallel diagonal changes D.

**The blocks of 100 zeros (the animation's 250 steps; `experiments/block_flow_and_prime_flow.py`).** Every block winds 49.3–50.5 turns (θ rises by π per zero), so the area a block sweeps does not halve. What shrinks is the new ground a block adds on a 40 × 40 map of the value plane: 443, 181, 120, 82, 66, 61, 42, 46, 40, 31 cells (ratios 0.41, 0.66, 0.68, 0.80, 0.92, 0.69, 1.10, 0.87). Each block covers about the same 463 cells; the union saturates at 1,598 of 1,600. The block's span in t follows 100·2π/ln(t/2π) (226.5, 159.9, 145.5, 137.9 …); its spread grows slowly (principal variance 1.48, 2.00, 2.19, 2.40 …) and its Re < 0 share rises (17.4%, 19.5%, 21.0%, 21.5% …): Selberg's slow broadening of log ζ.

**The flow of the primes, and what it is.**
- *Landau's formula* (1911; Gonek 1993): Σ_{0<γ≤T} cos(γ ln n) = −(T/2π) Λ(n)/√n + O(log T) for a prime power n. The zeros, as frequencies, spike at every ln p^k with weight ln p / p^(k/2). Measured / predicted, mean |ratio − 1| over the prime powers n < 60: 0.138 at 100 zeros, 0.0019 at 25,000 (n = 2: 0.945, 1.003, 0.998, 1.001 at 100, 1,000, 5,000, 25,000 zeros).
- *The same fact in the zero positions.* S(T) = N(T) − θ(T)/π − 1 = (1/π) arg ζ(½+iT) is the phase wobble of the pathway. S(T) ≈ −(1/π) Σ_{p,k} sin(kT ln p)/(k p^(k/2)) (Selberg 1946; Titchmarsh §14). Regressing S at the 24,999 gaps on sin and cos of T ln n for prime powers n ≤ 200 explains **99.6%** of its variance; the fitted sin amplitudes are −0.218, −0.171, −0.122, −0.096, −0.068, −0.059 for p = 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 against −0.225, −0.184, −0.142, −0.120, −0.096, −0.088 predicted (97%, 93%, 86%, 80%, 70%, 67%; the sum is truncated, which Selberg's smoothing weights explain); the cosine amplitudes are ~10⁻⁵. Var S = 0.050 here, against 0.117 for the bare leading term ln ln T/(2π²) (the constant term matters at this height).
- *So the flow is:* the primes enter the pathway as the phase wobble. The path turns by θ(t) (smooth, ≈ ½ t ln(t/2π e)); the zeros fall where θ(t)/π + 1 + S(t) crosses an integer, and S is the sum of prime waves of frequency ln p^k and amplitude 1/(π k p^(k/2)). Dual statement: the spectrum of the zeros has spikes at ln p^k with weights Λ(n)/√n. This is the explicit formula in its two faces.
- *An error of mine, corrected:* the first spectrum panel sampled Σcos(γx) on a grid of 1,800 points; at T ≈ 22,000 the spikes are about 4.5×10⁻⁵ wide, far narrower than the grid, so it mostly missed them. The page now evaluates Landau's sums at x = ln n exactly.

**The boundary-engine images (all viewed).** W1/O3 strips: concentric rosette rings; O2 strips: the worm, a tight core with one long arm; R3 strips: a pinwheel that goes to a star and back to a plain spiral at τ = 1; Z1 strips: the ζ Euler path, a spiral of circles with a scribbled core that, at τ = 1, sits displaced from the origin with a jagged tail (the later, smaller prime steps). Transitions G1→Z1 and O2→Z1 (cartesian, polar, fallback) all end at α = 1 on the identical Z1 image (concentric circles about a displaced centre, jagged tail to the right), by different roads: G1 through a chain of trochoid curls, O2 through a contracting spiral with crescents; O2→O3 and R2→R3 end on dense rosettes/pinwheels instead. That is the "different spiral pathways ending on the same object": Z1 is the prime spring S, whose loops are uniform in ln p with spacing 2π/t.

## 9. Third pass: forbidden zone, running ζ through the attractor, precession and twin primes, the y axis, a reservoir test (2026-11-24; `experiments/attractor_zeta_coupling.py`, `reservoir_prime_flow.py`)

**Is there a forbidden zone, an asymptote?** No hole in the middle: the path goes through the origin at every zero (the t-share with |ζ| < ε is 0.52 ε, a line through 0, not a depleted disc) and the sparse side is thin, not empty (about 0.23 of average density at ±180°). There is a **forbidden exterior**: writing ζ as the Riemann–Siegel sum gives |ζ(½+it)| ≤ 2 Σ_{n≤N} n^(−½) + (t/2π)^(−¼), N = ⌊√(t/2π)⌋, which grows like 4(t/2π)^¼. The path never leaves it; the largest value in 25,000 zeros is 19.8 (at t = 14,304) against a bound of 24.8 there, 91% at the closest approach (block maxima reach 0.91 of the bound at small t, 0.83 later). The true growth rate is not known (Lindelöf's hypothesis, unproven, says t^ε); the bound is the asymptote that is certain. **Asymptotes of the chart:** in a log-radius chart (the UNS ρ = log₁₀|ζ| + 3) every zero is a vertical asymptote, ln|ζ| ≈ ln|ζ′(ρ)| + ln|t − γ|; near a zero the path is a straight line through 0 with speed |ζ′(ρ)| (0.79 to 7.7, median 2.85, first 300 zeros). **What happens at the near-pinches:** between zeros 4,765 and 4,766 (t ≈ 5,229.2, a gap of 0.043 against a mean gap of 0.877) the path crosses 0, turns and comes back, never getting further than 0.0032 from 0 (a Lehmer pair; the next: zeros 11,204 and 6,709 at 0.004). A double zero would be a true cusp; none is known. In Aizawa the forbidden zone is the axis: the hole at the centre of the pole view (r > 0 always).

**Can ζ be run through the attractor, or the attractor through ζ?** Yes at the skeleton, and only partly beyond it.
- *Skeleton (exact).* Aizawa's first two equations are dφ/dt = d and dr/dt = (z − b) r. Put d := −θ′(t) and (z − b) := Z′/Z and they integrate to r e^(iφ) = ζ(½+it) exactly (Hardy). ζ is the attractor's skeleton driven by its zeros instead of by the third equation. The chaos is in the third equation, which ζ does not use.
- *The orbit read as the s-plane.* With s = x + 19 i y the orbit covers t from −27.6 to 29.0 and the plane x = ½ is the critical line; it is crossed 877 times; the first three zeros (±14.13, ±21.02, ±25.01 i) are visited within 0.004–0.115 in projection (z is free). The heights at the crossings are not clustered near the zero heights (z = −0.35 against a null that shifts the zero heights; my first null was wrong and gave −6.6). Painted by log₁₀|ζ(x + 19iy)| the orbit ranges −1.50 to +0.83; the colour is uncorrelated with z (0.001) and correlated with the orbit radius (0.52). The page has this under "paint the attractor with ζ".
- *Aizawa with ζ's rotation rate.* d = θ′(t) at t = 14.13, 236.5, 1,000, 6,890, 21,942 gives d = 0.41, 1.81, 2.53, 3.50, 4.08 and largest Lyapunov exponent 0.018, 0.0073, 0.013, **0.085**, 0.0033. θ′(t) equals Aizawa's d = 3.5 only at t = 2π e⁷ = 6,890.35, after zero 6,581: **block 66 (zeros 6,501–6,600)**, marked on the page. Aizawa's chaos is strong only near its own d.

**The two precessions, and the twin primes.** At f = 0 the (r, z) flow is planar, so it settles on a limit cycle (period 6.0493, z_max 1.7807 with no spread) and the 3-D orbit is a torus with rotation number d/ω_rz = 3.3697 (fractional part 0.3697: the loops precess 2.32 rad per (r, z) cycle). At f = 0.1 the torus is broken: z maxima 5.87 ± 0.36 apart, angle advance per cycle 1.69 ± 1.27 rad mod 2π, a broad z-spectrum around ω ≈ 1.07, rotation number 3.27. Tests of the standard forms of a twin-prime link, all null: none of the five frequency ratios is within 1% of any of nine twin-prime-type constants (C₂ = 0.66016…, 2C₂, B₂ = 1.90216…, ln 2 …; 0.45 hits expected by chance, 0 found); sampling the orbit at twin primes n (1,614 of them below 60,000) gives phase concentration R = 0.004, Rayleigh p = 0.97 (lone primes p = 0.85, all integers p = 0.997), z distribution against lone primes MWU p = 0.16, KS p = 0.089. I do not know your theory; these are the forms that could have caught it. Tell me the form and I will test it.

**If ½ is an x offset, what is the y offset?** s ↦ s + δ multiplies every term n^(−s) by n^(−δ). A real δ (an x move) is a **dilation** of every integer's term, n^(−Δσ); an imaginary δ = iτ (a y move) is a **rotation** of each integer's phasor by −τ ln n, a twist that differs for every integer. So x is the amplitude axis and y is the phase axis, conjugate to ln n by Fourier duality; on σ = ½ the functional-equation factor is a pure phase and the series and its mirror have equal amplitude. **Shadows** (images under ζ): of the vertical lines σ = 0.3, 0.4, ½, 0.6, 0.7, 1 the minimum |ζ| over t ≤ 60 is 0.174, 0.087, **0.008**, 0.080, 0.148, 0.326: only σ = ½ reaches the origin. Of the horizontal lines Im s = ½, 1, π/2, π, 2π the minimum |ζ| over σ ∈ [−3, 4] is 0.0106, 0.0177, 0.033, 0.145, 0.956: the near-misses are the trivial zero at s = −2 (|ζ′(−2)| = ζ(3)/4π² = 0.0304 times the height), and the circle numbers π and 2π stay far from 0. A horizontal line meets a non-trivial zero only at Im s = γ.

**A reservoir test of the attractor as the orbweaver's step function** (words → primes by `prime_hash`, phase ln p and the windspeed γ added to the state; 8-way task, 30,000 Brown words; gain over the majority rate): the attractor state returns the word seen now and one step ago (3 features: +25.3, +7.3 points; 9 features: +30.4, +14.5) and nothing beyond (lag 2: +0.5 to +1.0; lag 3 and over: ≤ 0.1, or negative from overfitting); shuffled labels give −3.4 (no leak). A plain **linear leaky integrator of the same size does better**: decay 0.3: +56.2, +28.2, +2.6; decay 0.5: +37.0, +17.7, +2.1; decay 0.7: +15.9, +8.8, +2.1 (lags 0, 1, 2). My first version of this test rotated the state by the kick and scored 16 classes; every reservoir scored 0, a flawed design kept as `reservoir_prime_flow_v1_flawed.json`. So this test gives **no support** for the attractor as a better memory than a leaky sum. It does not show the attractor is useless as an orbweaver: other couplings, longer steps and bigger readouts are untried, and the orbweaver's own task has not been stated as a test.

## 10. The orange slice, the through-the-sphere chart, and the nested slices (`experiments/wedge_and_interior.py`)

- **What the sparse area is.** On the value sphere it is the large-|ζ| end of the Re < 0 side: of the points with |ζ| > 1, 18.1% have Re ζ < 0; for |ζ| > 3, 14.1%; for |ζ| > 6, 9.6% (near the origin it is 31.6%, since the path crosses 0 in every direction). The left/right density ratio is 0.37, 0.16, 0.11, 0.05 in latitude bands from the south (small |ζ|) to the north cap. Only 10 of the 2,592 cells of an azimuth × latitude map are empty, so the slice is a depletion, not a hole.
- **Lobes.** Each of the 24,998 loops between consecutive zeros has its peak within 120° of +Re (15,797 within 30°, 7,470 at 30–60°, 1,625 at 60–90°, 106 at 90–120°, none beyond), and the median peak shrinks from 1.71 (toward +Re) to 0.56 (90–120°), where 80% never leave the southern hemisphere: they turn round before the equator |ζ| = 1.
- **The frontier is a limaçon.** The farthest |ζ| in each 10° direction after 25,000 zeros fits r(φ) = 13.34 + 7.00 cos φ (R² = 0.977; an offset-disc law fits worse, 0.931): b/a = 1.9, a smooth pseudo-cardioid with no cusp, reaching 19.8 toward +Re and 4.6 toward −Re.
- **Mechanism (partly verified).** ζ = S + e^(−2iθ) S̄ + R, |ζ| ≤ 2|S|, direction at an exact peak = arg S; S = 1 + (n ≥ 2 terms) has mean 1, so large |S| is mostly 1 plus aligned terms. Not verified: the lobe-peak direction equals arg S only roughly (median error 0.52 rad), and arg S itself is beyond 120° about 8.6% of the time, while no lobe peaks there; so the empty direction range is a size-selection effect (a large |S| needs aligned terms), not simply the distribution of arg S. A Gaussian model S = 1 + CN(0, Σ1/n) gives 6–13% beyond 120° and does not reproduce the 0 of 24,998.
- **Through the sphere.** The interior chart takes u = ζ/(1+|ζ|) as the horizontal position and w = tanh(Z′/2Z) as the third coordinate (Aizawa's x, y, z analogue; z is the radial driver). The path crosses the unit sphere constantly: 31.7% of it inside (32.5% t-weighted), 68% outside, median radius 1.03 (5–95%: 0.65–1.21); w changes sign twice per zero (1.997); each zero is a vertical jump through the centre. 3,431 of the 7,208 cells of the unit ball are visited (48%), and in the left wedge (|arg| > 138°) only 12 of 1,644 (0.7%): there the slice is almost empty.
- **Nested slices.** Cumulative frontier after N zeros (N = 100 … 25,000): reach toward +Re 6.1, 7.6, 9.2, 10.5, 13.4, 14.8, 16.5, 19.8 (log-log slope 0.255 against t, the t^¼ of the bound); toward −Re 0.01, 0.35, 1.4, 1.5, 2.3, 3.2, 5.1, 5.1 (slope 1.05, in jumps); wedge width 170° → 110°. Per window of 1,000 zeros the Re < 0 share rises 0.207 → 0.251 and the left/right density 0.054 → 0.125, linear in ln ln t (R² 0.90, 0.88); the wedge-width trend is noisy (R² 0.52). Block to block there is no alternation (residual sd 0.009, lag autocorrelations −0.19, 0.13, −0.04, −0.02, 0.01).
- **spirals.png** (`rhp/atlas_riemann_fermat/spirals.py`) draws ζ(½+it) as a helix about the t axis whose radius is the signed Z(t): it passes through the axis at the zeros, and for σ = ½, 0.75, 1 the axis becomes a widening hole (min |ζ| 0.000, 0.180, 0.326). That is the interior curve the through-the-sphere chart shows in the value coordinates.

## 11. The attractor in UNS maths (`experiments/aizawa_in_uns.py`; panel "Aizawa and ζ in UNS" and the Aizawa chart "UNS: ln r, φ, z" on the page)

- **Complex form.** w = x + iy obeys dw/dt = (z − b + i d) w exactly (finite-difference residual 2×10⁻⁴ over 300,000 steps). In log-polar coordinates u = ln r, φ = arg w: du/dt = z − b, dφ/dt = d; only dz/dt = c + az − z³/3 − e^(2u)(1 + ez) + f z e^(3u) cos³φ feeds back. φ is a pure rotation; u integrates the driver.
- **A torus.** The orbit lies on a surface of revolution about the axis whose cross-section is the (u, z) curve: at f = 0 the closed limit cycle (u −1.678 … 0.398, z −0.266 … 1.781, period 6.0493), at f = 0.1 a filled band (u −2.055 … 0.397, z −0.361 … 1.835; 752 section points per 300,000 steps; correlation −0.007). The axis is forbidden: r ≥ 0.116 (u ≥ −2.15), r ≤ 1.53.
- **The rate plane.** λ = d(ln w)/dt = (z − b) + i d is a horizontal segment: Re from −1.066 to 1.148, mean −0.0003 (u is bounded, hence ⟨z⟩ = b), Im = 3.5; the radius grows 47.7% of the time.
- **ζ in the same chart.** ζ = e^(−iθ) Z gives λ = Z′/Z − iθ′(t): the imaginary part drifts 0.23 → 4.08 (equal to Aizawa's d at t = 6,890) and the real part is unbounded (22.2% of the path has |Z′/Z| > 10: the poles at the zeros). On the section arg ζ = 0 (12,283 crossings, near the Gram points) ζ is always near a zero: median |ζ| e^(−1.92) = 0.15, median rate 34.5: in ζ the phase is locked to the radial driver (Gram's law and the interlacing of Gram points and zeros), in Aizawa the phase φ = d t is independent of u. That is the structural difference the panel shows: a bounded ring against unbounded horseshoes.
- **The reference plane.** The x = ½ frame had no meaning inside Aizawa (it only held in the s = x + 19iy reading, still available as a checkbox). ## 12. The section as a shape, and the Mexican hat (`experiments/section_void_test.py`, `mexican_hat_test.py`)

## 13. The project's hat, the Higgs numbers, and the lobes as circles (`experiments/hat_curvature_tests.py`; run with `ValaQuenta/.venv`, which has the PDG `particle` package)

- **Pair correlation of the zeros against the hat.** `witches_hat` takes the hat potential to be the fermion exchange hole 1 − (sin πu/πu)², which is also the Montgomery–Odlyzko pair correlation of the zeros (ESTABLISHED; the reading of the hat as that waveform is the author's, THEORETICAL). The empirical R₂(u) of the 25,000 unfolded zeros matches it with RMSE 0.041 (60 bins to u = 3); the hole exponent is 1.84 for u < 0.35 (expected 2, coarse bins); the first maximum is at u = 0.875 against the brim at 1.0 (bin width 0.05). So the zeros' repulsion hole is the project's hat, measured.
- **Aizawa's z-well against the hat shapes.** Normalised from the hilltop (z = −0.838) to the brim (z = 1.943), the tilted well is within RMSE 0.071 of the quartic n = 4 hat (the Higgs shape) and 0.197 of the sine-kernel hat: Aizawa's well is a Higgs-type quartic, not the zeros' hat. In linear-sigma-model terms λ = 1/3, μ² = a = 0.95, explicit-breaking term H = c = 0.6, vacuum σ₀ = 1.943, m_σ² = 2.83, H/σ₀ = 0.309. The tilt c = 0.6 is 97.2% of the saddle-node value (2/3)a^(3/2) = 0.617, where the left dimple disappears: the standard Aizawa parameters sit just inside a fold.
- **Higgs numbers** (`lagrangian_sm`): m_h = 125.13 GeV, v = 246.22 GeV, λ = m_h²/2v² = 0.1291, μ² = λv² = 7,829 GeV², m_h/v = 0.508. d* = W(1)/ln 10 = 0.246307 is 0.036% from v = 0.246220 TeV: flagged as a coincidence, not tested for meaning. Any quartic double well is the Higgs shape up to scale; the only invariant of Aizawa's well is its tilt τ = c/((2/3)a^(3/2)) = 0.972 (the SM has τ = 0).
- **Lobes are circles through the origin.** Fitting |ζ|² = 2 Re(ζ c̄) to each lobe (the path between consecutive zeros; 4,192 lobes above t ≈ 300, every fifth): median R² = **0.982**, 10–90%: 0.860–0.999, 93.6% above 0.8, 78.0% above 0.95. The fitted centre c is near the prime-spring endpoint S(t_mid): |c|/|S| median 0.93 (IQR 0.70–1.05), angle between them median 0.42 rad (55.5% within 0.5 rad). Not exact, because S moves during a lobe. This is the n = 1-type circle ζ ≈ |S|(1 + e^(−2iθ)) of §8 made quantitative: ζ = S + e^(−2iθ) S̄ is a family of circles r = 2|c| cos(φ − arg c) whose envelope is the limaçon frontier. Under the inversion ζ → 1/ζ̄ (the reflection through the equator |ζ| = 1) a circle through 0 is a straight line, so in the inverted chart the pathway is a chain of straight lines.
- **Curvature on the sphere.** A circle on the value plane is a circle on the sphere, so the lobes have (nearly) constant geodesic curvature there; measured coefficient of variation of κ_g along a lobe: median 0.29 (finite differences on jittered samples, noisy). The round sphere has K = +1 and the through-the-sphere chart is flat (K = 0) by construction, so no intrinsic curvature of a "curved space" was found or tested; what was found is that the pathway is piecewise circular.
