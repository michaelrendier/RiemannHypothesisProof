# The bit flip — the slot bit computed two ways   2026-11-10   :CALCULATED (no claim about RH)

## What sets the bit (ESTABLISHED)
Bit j of the `char` slot of a prime p is 1 exactly when the Kronecker symbol (d_j/p) = −1, p inert in ℚ(√d_j): p stays a hole there. d = −4, 8, −3, 5, −7, −11, 13. Two independent routes to it, for any odd n:
- **exponent:** Euler's criterion, d^((n−1)/2) mod n (one modular exponentiation);
- **reciprocity:** the Jacobi symbol (d/n) by the Euclid-style algorithm (quadratic reciprocity and the supplements for −1 and 2; no exponent, no factoring).
They agree for every prime (Euler's criterion). For a composite n they agree on base d only if n is an Euler–Jacobi pseudoprime to d (Solovay–Strassen): the slot vector computed two ways is a built-in complementarity check. Vectorised implementations (Jacobi checked against sympy on 20,000 random pairs, modular power against Python `pow`: 0 mismatches).

## Results (all 5,000,000 odd n in [3, 10⁷]; 664,578 odd primes, 4,335,422 composites)
- **Primes:** all 664,578 agree on all seven bases (0 false rejections).
- **Composites agreeing, per base:** −4: 750 (the Fermat base-2 count, as it must: (−1/n) = (−1)^((n−1)/2) makes this base the base-2 Fermat test), 8: 1009, −3: 413, 5: 348, −7: 304, −11: 280, 13: 313.
- **Survivors of the first r bases in the listed order:** 750, 375, 77, 35, 14, 6, 3.
- **Best subsets by size (fewest surviving composites):** 1 base {−11}: 280; 2 bases {−11, 13}: 21; 3 bases {5, −7, −11}: 7; 4 bases: 4; 5, 6 and 7 bases: **3**.
- **Zero survivors:** two bases {5, 13} are exact below 10⁵; three bases {−4, −11, 13} are exact below 10⁶; **no subset of the seven is exact to 10⁷**: three composites agree on every base, 1,857,241 = 31·181·331, 2,433,601 = 17·37·53·73 and 6,189,121 = 61·241·421. All three are Carmichael numbers (squarefree, (p−1) | (n−1) for every factor) and all are ≡ 1 mod 8.
- For comparison, from `experiments/min_primality_steps.json`: the strong (Miller–Rabin) tests with bases 2, 3, 5 are exact to 10⁷. Per exponentiation the Euler–Jacobi slot screen is weaker than the strong test, as theory says (a strong pseudoprime to a base is an Euler pseudoprime to it, not the reverse).

## Reading
1. **The indicator light is cheap and one-sided:** each bit costs one exponentiation or one Euclid, a prime is never rejected, and the screen thins the composites by the factors above (4.3 million composites → 750 → 375 → 77 → 35 → 14 → 6 → 3).
2. **The two-route disagreement is the information:** it is zero for holes (primes) and non-zero for most of the bulk, which is the complementarity check you asked for between the exponent side and the reciprocity side.
3. **What survives seven bits is a specific kind of composite:** three Carmichael numbers below 10⁷, all with every prime factor inert or split in a way that the seven characters cannot separate from a prime. They are the stragglers of this screen.
4. The seven fixed bits do not make a complete test; a complete one needs either more bases (the screen is a probabilistic Solovay–Strassen with fixed bases) or the strong test's extra square-root-of-1 step.
