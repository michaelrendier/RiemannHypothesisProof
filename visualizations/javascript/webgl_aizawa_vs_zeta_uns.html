Complex form. w = x + iy obeys dw/dt = (z − b + i d) w exactly. In log-polar coordinates u = ln r, φ = arg w: du/dt = z − b, dφ/dt = d, and only dz/dt = c + a z − z³/3 − e^(2u)(1 + e z) + f z e^(3u) cos³φ feeds back. So (u, φ, z) is the native chart: φ is a pure rotation, u integrates the driver, and the orbit lies on a torus (a surface of revolution about the axis) whose cross-section is the (u, z) curve: at f = 0 the closed limit cycle (u from −1.68 to 0.40, z from −0.27 to 1.78, period 6.05), at f = 0.1 a filled band (u from −2.06 to 0.40, z from −0.36 to 1.83, correlation −0.007 on the φ = 0 section, 752 crossings in 300,000 steps).
Rate plane. λ = d(ln w)/dt = (z − b) + i d: a horizontal segment, Re from −1.07 to 1.15 with mean −0.0003 (u is bounded, so ⟨z⟩ = b), Im = 3.5. The axis is forbidden: r ≥ 0.116 (u ≥ −2.15).
ζ in the same chart. ζ = e−iθ Z, so λ = Z′/Z − i θ′(t): the imaginary part drifts from 0.23 to 4.08 (it equals Aizawa's d at t = 6,890) and the real part is unbounded (22% of the path has |Z′/Z| > 10: poles at the zeros). On the section arg ζ = 0 (near the Gram points) ζ is always near a zero (median |ζ| 0.15, median rate 34): in ζ the phase is locked to the radial driver, in Aizawa it is not. Test of the section as the unreachable shape (wide view): the ζ section is a thin hyperbola (corr −0.835 between ln radius and asinh rate; rate·|ζ| = |Z′| median 5.1; 99.95% on the positive branch), and the whole path spends 82.6% of its time in the cells the section leaves empty, so the section's void is not the forbidden region. The forbidden region of the ζ portrait is the area beyond the hyperbola |Z′| = |rate|·|ζ| = 30.9 (the fastest speed through a zero in 25,000: median 5.3, slowest 0.24) and below the speed floor near the axis: together they explain 1,480 of the 1,754 empty cells (84%). Aizawa's forbidden region is the hole inside its ring (44 enclosed cells, 0 points from the section or the full orbit).
Is there a sonic boom? Define the Mach number of the rate plane as M = |Re λ|/|Im λ| (radial growth rate over rotation rate: below 1 the path winds, above 1 it plunges). Aizawa: M ≤ 0.328, always subsonic (a hard ceiling: |z − b| ≤ 1.148, speed ≤ 5.58); with d lowered it becomes supersonic below d ≈ 1.1 (1.5% of the orbit at d = 1.1, 20% at 1.0, 84% at 0.3), with no sharp signature in the Lyapunov exponent or the ring. ζ is supersonic exactly half its time (0.4999) and its Mach number is Cauchy-distributed: P(M < m) = (2/π) arctan m (0.1562, 0.2951, 0.5001, 0.7047, 0.8443, 0.9370 measured against 0.1560, 0.2952, 0.5000, 0.7048, 0.8440, 0.9365 at m = 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 10), the law of |tan| of a uniform phase. The density is smooth across M = 1: no shock at the barrier. The one singular event is each zero, where M → ∞ and ln|ζ| → −∞. Ceilings: the speed through a zero, |Z′(γ)|, has no fixed ceiling: its largest value is 5.1, 7.7, 11.7, 15.5, 22.0, 30.9 in the zero ranges 0–100, 100–300, 300–1,000, 1,000–3,000, 3,000–10,000, 10,000–25,000, the mean square follows Gonek's (ln T)³ law up to a factor 2.9 falling to 1.7, and the largest path speed seen is 78.4. 31 is the cutoff of the data, not of the function.
Curvature. At a zero the path's curvature is exactly κ = −2θ′/|Z′| (median ratio 1.0000), so the lobe is a circle of radius |Z′|/(2θ′) (median 0.74); all ζ curvature is clockwise (median |κ| 0.97). Aizawa: space-curve curvature median 1.45 (5–95%: 0.72–2.86, max 3.63), torsion median 0.20 (−0.21 … 2.15); in the UNS torus chart curvature 0.77 (0.54–1.26) and torsion −0.02 (nearly planar). Under inversion the lobes are straight lines of the flat metric ds² = |dζ|²/|ζ|⁴, whose origin is at infinite distance: each lobe is a complete geodesic from ∞ to ∞.
Is it a Mexican hat? Partly. Aizawa's z-equation is exactly minus the slope of a tilted quartic double well, dz/dt = −∂V/∂z with V = z⁴/12 − a z²/2 − c z + r² z + (e/2) r² z² (residual 4×10⁻¹⁰): minima at z = 1.943 (V = −1.77, deep) and z = −1.105 (V = 0.207, a shallow dimple), hump at −0.838 (V = 0.210); the orbit (z from −0.37 to 1.85) lives in the deep well and reaches the lip of the hump. But dr/dt = (z − b) r is a growth law, not −∂V/∂r, so it is not a gradient flow. In the plane the Aizawa density has sombrero topology (empty hole r < 0.116, ring peak at r = 0.29), and the f z x³ term thickens the ring inward (r from 0.187 to 0.116). ζ has no hat: its value-plane density rises as 1/|ζ| to the origin (fitted slope −1.002, no hole), which has the form of the 2-D point-mass potential ln r but follows from crossing 0 at nonzero speed, so it is not evidence of gravity or curvature. The z = b plane (gold) is Aizawa's radial balance, where d ln r/dt = 0.

What the pole view shows. The path leans to Re ζ > 0: the mean value of ζ(½+it) over t is 1 (the n = 1 term), and the n = 1 term alone is a circle through 0 that never enters Re < 0. The primes and prime powers (n ≥ 2) push the path into Re < 0 for about 24% of its length at 25,000 zeros (18% at 100, 21% at 1,000, 23% at 5,000), so between a fifth and a quarter of the picture is the sparse side, centred on the negative real axis at every zero count. It is not a truncation: it is the width of log ζ, which Selberg (1946) showed grows only like √(½ ln ln t), so the sparse side fills very slowly with height. Exact structure: ζ = S + e−2iθ S̄ + R with S = Σn≤N n−½−it, N = ⌊√(t/2π)⌋: the prime spring plus its mirror turning at −2θ′, a pseudo-cardioid with no cusp (|Z′| ≥ 0.62 at the first 1,500 zeros).

The orange slice, measured. On the value sphere the slice is not empty but it is cut from the large-|ζ| end: of the points with |ζ| > 6 only 9.6% have Re ζ < 0 (18% for |ζ| > 1), and the left/right density ratio falls from 0.37 near the origin to 0.05 near ∞. Every one of the 24,998 lobes (the loop between two zeros) has its peak within 120° of +Re: 15,797 within 30°, 7,470 at 30–60°, 1,625 at 60–90°, 106 at 90–120°, none beyond. The farthest the path reaches in each direction, the frontier (yellow), is a limaçon, r(φ) ≈ 13.3 + 7.0 cos φ (R² = 0.977, b/a = 1.9: a pseudo-cardioid, smooth, no cusp): 19.8 toward +Re and 4.6 toward −Re. The slice is its dimple. Reason: ζ = S + e−2iθ S̄ with S = 1 + (n ≥ 2 terms) and |ζ| ≤ 2|S|; the fixed first term 1 puts the centre of S on +1, so a large |S| is mostly 1 plus aligned terms. In the through-the-sphere chart (ζ compressed to a disc, and the driver Z′/Z as the third coordinate, Aizawa's x, y, z analogue) the path crosses the unit sphere: 32% of it inside, 68% outside, median radius 1.03, and the left wedge (|arg| > 138°) is almost empty: 12 of its 1,644 cells are visited, against 48% of the whole ball. Each zero is a vertical line through the centre of the sphere.

The slices, nested. Taking the first N zeros, the frontier grows outward and the slice outside it shrinks at every checkpoint. N = 100, 200, 500, 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, 10,000, 25,000: reach toward +Re 6.1, 7.6, 9.2, 10.5, 13.4, 14.8, 16.5, 19.8 (growing like t0.255, the t¼ of the Riemann–Siegel bound); reach toward −Re 0.01, 0.35, 1.4, 1.5, 2.3, 3.2, 5.1, 5.1 (faster but in jumps: nothing new between 10,000 and 25,000 zeros); wedge width (where the frontier is under half the +Re reach) 170°, 165°, 155°, 140°, 140°, 130°, 110°, 110°. Per window of 1,000 zeros the Re < 0 share rises 0.207 → 0.251 and the left/right density 0.054 → 0.125, both linear in ln ln t (R² 0.90, 0.88): Selberg's slow broadening. Block to block there is no alternating pattern (residual spread 0.009, autocorrelations about 0).

The flow of the primes, and what the maths say it is. Landau's formula (1911; Gonek 1993): for a prime power n = pk, Σ0<γ≤T cos(γ ln n) = −(T/2π) Λ(n)/√n + O(log T). The zeros, as a set of frequencies, carry a spike at every ln pk with weight ln p/pk/2; the bars show it converging as zeros are added (mean deviation from the prediction 0.138 at 100 zeros, 0.0019 at 25,000, for the prime powers below 60). The same fact in the zero positions: the fluctuation of the zero count, S(T) = N(T) − θ(T)/π − 1 = (1/π) arg ζ(½+iT), is a sum of prime waves, S(T) ≈ −(1/π) Σp,k sin(kT ln p)/(k pk/2) (Selberg 1946; Titchmarsh §14). Regressing S at the 24,999 gaps on sin and cos of T ln n for the prime powers n ≤ 200 explains 99.6% of its variance; the fitted amplitudes for p = 2, 3, 5, 7 are 97%, 93%, 86%, 80% of the predicted −1/(π k pk/2), falling for larger p because the sum is truncated (Selberg's smoothing). So the primes flow through the zeros as the phase wobble of the pathway: each block of 100 zeros turns 50 times (θ rises by π per zero), and the wobble in where the zeros fall is the primes.

The blocks. Each block of 100 zeros winds 49.3 to 50.5 turns, so the area it sweeps does not halve. What shrinks is the new ground it adds to the blocks before: 443, 181, 120, 82, 66, 61, 42, 46, 40, 31 cells of a 40 × 40 map of the value plane (ratios 0.41, 0.66, 0.68, 0.80, 0.92, …). The blocks repeat one footprint, the pseudo-cardioid, and the union saturates (1,598 of 1,600 cells); the block's spread grows slowly with height.

Both are a rotation with a radial driver. Aizawa: dφ/dt = d, dr/dt = (z − b) r, never reaching the axis. ζ = e−iθ(t)·Z(t): angle −θ(t), signed radius Z; Z′/Z is the sum of the zero poles 1/(t − γ). In the cylinder chart the zeros sit on the axis at height γ; in the sphere chart every zero is the south pole. Prime ticks mark where prime p's phasor is back at angle 0 (t·ln p = 2πk).
