What the pole view shows. The path leans to Re ζ > 0: the mean value of ζ(½+it) over t is 1 (the n = 1 term), and the n = 1 term alone is a circle through 0 that never enters Re < 0. The primes and prime powers (n ≥ 2) push the path into Re < 0 for about 24% of its length at 25,000 zeros (18% at 100, 21% at 1,000, 23% at 5,000), so between a fifth and a quarter of the picture is the sparse side, centred on the negative real axis at every zero count. It is not a truncation: it is the width of log ζ, which Selberg (1946) showed grows only like √(½ ln ln t), so the sparse side fills very slowly with height. Exact structure: ζ = S + e−2iθ S̄ + R with S = Σn≤N n−½−it, N = ⌊√(t/2π)⌋: the prime spring plus its mirror turning at −2θ′, a pseudo-cardioid with no cusp (|Z′| ≥ 0.62 at the first 1,500 zeros).

Both are a rotation with a radial driver. Aizawa: dφ/dt = d, dr/dt = (z − b) r, never reaching the axis. ζ = e−iθ(t)·Z(t): angle −θ(t), signed radius Z; Z′/Z is the sum of the zero poles 1/(t − γ). In the cylinder chart the zeros sit on the axis at height γ; in the sphere chart every zero is the south pole. Prime ticks mark where prime p's phasor is back at angle 0 (t·ln p = 2πk).
